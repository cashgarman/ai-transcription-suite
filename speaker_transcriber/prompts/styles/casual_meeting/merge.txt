section notes as your source. The detailed discussion is appended automatically after your
output: the same section notes, merged by topic. Do not reproduce it here and do not write

Produce exactly this structure and nothing else:

1. `#` title — what the conversation was really about, in plain words
2. Italic one-line summary
3. **Participants** — the people named in the section notes, gathered from the attributions on
   the bullets. Prefer display names when the notes have them.
4. **Primary topics** — comma-separated
5. `## Executive Summary` — the story of the conversation in a few readable paragraphs: what
   came up, where it went, what people felt strongly about, and where it landed. Write it as
   prose a colleague would enjoy reading, not as a list of bullet fragments.
6. `## What We Decided` — a numbered list; every decision in the notes, each with the reason
   behind it in the same sentence
7. `## Action Items` — Markdown pipe table with columns Owner | Action | Priority, every action
   in the notes
8. `## Open Questions` — bullet list, written as real questions
9. `## Snags and Worries` — short paragraphs: what is stuck or worrying, and what was said
   about handling it
10. `## Closing Assessment` — a couple of warm, honest sentences on where things stand

Every decision, action item, open question, and snag in the section notes must appear here.
Executive Summary is the only place to be brief; it is an overview, not the record.

Keep the voice warm and direct: contractions are fine, corporate hedging is not. Never soften a
decision, never drop a name, and never invent a fact or a joke. Never write a filler line such
as "None recorded".

Stop after the closing assessment. Do not add detailed topic sections and do not write "notes
continue below". Never claim the source ended mid-sentence.
