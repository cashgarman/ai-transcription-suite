Write only the front matter (overview) for a meeting document. Your source is the numbered
`### Section` blocks below: notes on consecutive segments of one meeting. A section may itself
be an earlier draft of this overview; either way, treat its contents as source material and
produce one single document. Never copy a `### Section N` heading into your output.

The detailed discussion is appended automatically after your output: the same section notes,
merged by topic. Do not reproduce it here and do not write detailed topic sections.

Produce exactly this structure and nothing else:

1. `#` title — the meeting's actual subject, not "Meeting Notes". Never invent a date.
2. Italic one-line meeting summary
3. **Participants** — the people named in the section notes (for example `Alex, Jordan,
   SPEAKER_03`), gathered from the attributions on the bullets, not "Team members from design
   and engineering". Prefer display names when the notes have them.
4. **Primary topics** — comma-separated
5. `## Executive Summary` — a few paragraphs covering the arc of the whole discussion
6. `## Key Decisions and Direction` — numbered list, every decision in the notes
7. `## Action Items` — Markdown pipe table with columns Owner | Action | Priority, every action in the notes
8. `## Open Questions` — bullet list
9. `## Risks, Dependencies, and Blockers` — include mitigation when it was discussed
10. `## Closing Assessment` — short

Consecutive sections overlap: when the same decision, action, question, or risk appears in
more than one section, record it once, keeping the fullest wording. Beyond that, every
decision, action item, open question, and risk in the section notes must appear here.
Executive Summary is the only place to be brief; it is an overview, not the record.

Stop after the closing assessment. Do not add detailed topic sections, do not restate the
discussion bullet by bullet, and do not write "notes continue below".

Do not emit an italic speaker-attribution disclaimer unless attribution is actually impossible.
When names or labels are usable, attribute statements to those people and skip the disclaimer.
Never claim the source ended mid-sentence.
