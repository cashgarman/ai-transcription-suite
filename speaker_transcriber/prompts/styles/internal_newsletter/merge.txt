Write the internal newsletter. Your output is the whole issue. Your source is the numbered
`### Section` blocks below: material pulled from consecutive segments of one discussion. A
section may itself be an earlier draft of the issue; either way, treat its contents as source
material and produce one single issue. Never copy a `### Section N` heading into your output.

Produce exactly this structure:

1. `#` headline — a real headline about the biggest thing that happened, not "Weekly Update".
2. An italic one-line standfirst: the issue in a sentence.
3. `## The Short Version` — two or three sentences a busy reader can stop after.
4. `## What Happened` — the body of the issue. Short paragraphs grouped by topic, with a `###`
   subheading per topic when there is more than one. Names in. Reasons in.
5. `## Decisions` — a numbered list; every decision in the source, with who made it and why.
6. `## Who Owns What` — a Markdown pipe table with columns Owner | Action | Priority.
7. `## Still Open` — bullets: unsettled arguments, blocked work, questions waiting on someone.
8. `## What's Next` — what happens between now and the next update, with dates that were given.
9. `## Shoutouts` — bullets, only for praise actually given or work the source shows was done.
   Omit the section entirely if there is none.

The sections overlap: when the same decision, owner, deadline, or item appears in more than one
section, record it once, keeping the fullest wording. Beyond that, every decision, owner,
deadline, and open item in the source must appear somewhere. The Short Version is the only
place to be brief.

Write plainly and candidly: no corporate hedging, no invented enthusiasm, no filler such as
"None recorded" under a heading. Never invent a name, number, date, or compliment.

Output Markdown only, starting directly with the `#` headline: no preamble and no code fence.
Do not append the raw discussion notes.
