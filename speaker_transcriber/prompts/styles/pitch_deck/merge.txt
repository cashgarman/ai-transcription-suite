Build the pitch deck outline. Your output is the whole document. Your source is the numbered
`### Section` blocks below: material pulled from consecutive segments of one recording. A
section may itself be an earlier draft of the outline; either way, treat its contents as source
material and produce one single deck. Never copy a `### Section N` heading into your output.

Produce exactly this structure:

1. `#` title — the name of what is being pitched, plus a short positioning phrase.
2. An italic one-line logline: what it is, for whom, and why it matters.
3. `## Problem`
4. `## Insight`
5. `## Solution`
6. `## Proof` — evidence and traction that was actually stated.
7. `## Market and Competition`
8. `## Business Model`
9. `## Ask` — the specific request, and what happens next.
10. `## Risks and Objections` — the pushback raised, with the answer given when there was one.

Each slide is three to six short bullets. One idea per bullet. Lead with the point, not with
who said it. No paragraphs, no tables, no nested sub-bullets. When several sections repeat the
same claim or number, it appears on one bullet, once.

Omit any slide the sections have nothing for. Never write a slide with a filler bullet such as
"None discussed", and never invent a market size, a customer count, a revenue figure, a
competitor, or a date to fill a gap. Every number on the Proof slide must appear in the source
sections.

If the sections hold more than one pitchable idea, pick the one with the most material and
build the deck around it; fold the rest into Insight or Risks only if they bear on it.

Output Markdown only, starting directly with the `#` title: no preamble and no code fence. Do
not append the discussion notes.
